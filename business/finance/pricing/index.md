# Pricing

Pricing is a product decision, not a finance decision. Most people get it wrong by thinking about what it costs to build rather than what it's worth to the buyer.

---

## The Three-Tier Model (Good / Better / Best)

Almost every successful SaaS uses this. Three tiers:
1. **Entry** — low friction, limited, gets people in the door
2. **Core** — where most customers should land, fully featured
3. **Premium** — high value, high touch, unlocks advanced capabilities

The middle tier is the real product. The entry tier exists to lower acquisition cost. The premium tier exists to make the middle tier look reasonable (anchoring).

Display them with the premium tier first (left to right: Premium → Core → Entry) or highlight the core tier as "Most Popular." Both are anchoring techniques.

---

## Anchoring

People don't know what something is worth in absolute terms. They know what it's worth relative to something else. The first number they see anchors their perception of value.

- Show the annual price per month (appears lower than the monthly price)
- Show the highest tier first
- Show what competitors charge (if you're cheaper or you offer more)

---

## Annual vs Monthly

Offer annual at ~2 months free (16% discount). This is the industry standard for a reason:
- Reduces churn dramatically
- Improves cash flow
- Customers who pay annually churn at a fraction of monthly customers

Always show annual as the default toggle.

---

## Psychological Pricing

- $99 not $100 — the left digit effect is real
- $97 and $97/mo feel handcrafted and dated — use clean numbers for SaaS
- For B2B (especially enterprise): round numbers ($500/mo, $1,000/mo) signal confidence

---

## Per Seat vs Flat Rate vs Usage-Based

| Model | Best for | Trap |
|-------|---------|------|
| Per seat | B2B collaboration tools | Customers hide shared logins |
| Flat rate | Simple tools, solopreneurs | Whales pay the same as small users |
| Usage-based | Infrastructure, APIs, volume tools | Hard to predict, churn when usage spikes cost |

Hybrid (flat base + usage overage) captures the benefits of both for most SaaS.

---

## The $10k/Month Math

Before picking a price, work backwards:

| Price | Customers needed |
|-------|-----------------|
| $10/mo | 1,000 |
| $50/mo | 200 |
| $100/mo | 100 |
| $500/mo | 20 |
| $1,000/mo | 10 |

Getting 1,000 customers is 100x harder than getting 10. Charge more, need fewer customers, deliver more value to each. The low-price-high-volume model requires distribution that most indie builders don't have.

---

## What Gets Overlooked

- **Freemium is not a pricing strategy, it's an acquisition strategy** — if your free tier is too good, paid conversion stays low. The free tier should make the paid tier obvious.
- **Don't discount to close** — sets a precedent, trains customers to wait for discounts, signals low confidence in your price.
- **Price changes are easier than you think** — grandfathering existing customers and raising prices for new customers is standard. Most customers don't churn over a price increase if the value is clear.
- **Add-ons beat upgrading tiers** — customers resist moving tiers (feels like a commitment). They'll buy add-ons readily. Consider what you can sell à la carte.
